# V01 T5 — separate owners, unified cloud recovery

Status: observed on two physical devices on 2026-10-23. Two independent
`NSPersistentCloudKitContainer` objects opened and exported successfully, but a
fresh independent peer recovered both logical graphs into one SQLite store.
This topology does not provide R01's separate cloud worlds under the tested
conditions. See the [procedure](../graphevo-2-v01-procedure.md),
[T0 control](v01-t0-2026-10-23.md), and
[T1 native rejection](v01-t1-2026-10-23.md).

## Writer observation

The writer ran from 20:12:41 to 20:14:46 UTC, including store opening and the
configured 120-second sampling window. A-001/alpha and A-002/beta were saved
only through owner A; B-001/gamma only through owner B. Both saves succeeded.
Initial and final snapshots contained exactly those respective rows:

| Local store | Initial rows | Final rows |
|---|---|---|
| A | A-001/alpha, A-002/beta | A-001/alpha, A-002/beta |
| B | B-001/gamma | B-001/gamma |

Each owner reported successful completed setup, import, and export events,
attributed to its distinct native store identifier. No completed event errors,
stage errors, or dropped events were recorded. In-progress events with
`succeeded = false` and no end date were not classified as failures.

No cross-store arrival appears in the retained initial or final writer
snapshots; a complete intermediate time series is not retained. This sampled
local separation did not establish cloud isolation. Both writers' public
record mappings named `com.apple.coredata.cloudkit.zone`; zone-name equality
alone was not used as the conclusion.

## Independent fresh-peer observation

After the completed writer report was retained, its process was explicitly
terminated. The iPad reader ran from 20:15:08 to 20:15:14 UTC, ending early on
exact fixture recovery within its maximum 120-second window.

The reader recovered all three exact rows in its single new SQLite store:
A-001/alpha, A-002/beta, and B-001/gamma. It performed no inserts and reported
`localSaveCompleted = false`, `exactFixtureRecovered = true`, and
`unifiedPeerReplicaObserved`. Both graph labels remained visible without a
graph-label predicate. This is positive evidence of a common recovery domain,
not an inference from missing rows or an export event alone.

All three record names and zone names matched the corresponding writer
mappings. The two writer stores and peer had three distinct local store
identifiers; writer and reader had different stage/process IDs and identical
source provenance. Reader setup/import/export events completed successfully,
with no stage/event errors or dropped events recorded.

## Design implication

Distinct persistent-container objects and SQLite files, with the same model,
iCloud destination, account and private scope, did not create independent cloud
recovery worlds in this trial. R01 remains a requirement, but this topology
does not satisfy it. Return the limitation to discussion before selecting an
architecture, replacing isolation with graph-label filtering, or changing the
synchronization stack. Different supported boundaries and shared graphs require
their own feasibility review; this result makes no claim about every possible
Core Data/CloudKit topology or future synchronization timing.
